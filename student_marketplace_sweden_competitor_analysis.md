# Sweden Competitor Analysis for Solo-Founder MVP

## Goal

Confirm whether students actually want a student-first marketplace and identify which first category has the strongest pain:

- Textbooks
- Dorm items
- Furniture
- Electronics

This analysis is focused on a solo founder trying to launch, float, and market an e-commerce application in Sweden.

## Rating Method

**Public rating** means a visible public rating found from Trustpilot or the company website.

**Category rating** is a practical 1-5 assessment of how strongly each competitor solves that specific student category:

- 1 = weak or mostly irrelevant
- 2 = possible but not focused
- 3 = moderate fit
- 4 = strong fit
- 5 = very strong/direct competitor

## Category Assessment

### Textbooks

Textbooks are painful for students because course books are expensive and needed every semester.

However, this category already has strong Sweden-specific competitors:

- Studentapan
- Campusbokhandeln
- Bokbörsen and general book marketplaces
- Studentlitteratur for new academic books

**Assessment:** painful, but not the best first wedge.

**Reason:** the problem is already clearly understood and already served by specialist competitors.

### Dorm Items

Dorm items include lamps, kitchen items, small appliances, storage, routers, bedding, small desks, and other practical student essentials.

This category is strongly connected to:

- Moving into student housing
- Moving out after a semester
- Low student budgets
- Temporary living situations
- Local pickup needs

Existing competitors such as Blocket and Facebook Marketplace support this category, but they are broad marketplaces. They are not student-first, campus-first, or timed around student move-in/move-out moments.

**Assessment:** very strong first wedge.

**Reason:** it has real pain, local urgency, and room for a trusted student-focused experience.

### Furniture

Furniture is one of the strongest second-hand categories in Sweden.

Blocket and Facebook Marketplace are very strong here, but the student pain remains specific:

- Students often need cheap furniture quickly.
- Transport is hard.
- Trust is uneven.
- Listings are not organized around campus or student areas.
- Move-in/move-out timing creates predictable supply and demand.

**Assessment:** strong, especially small furniture.

**Reason:** big furniture may be operationally hard for a solo founder, but small furniture is practical and highly relevant.

### Electronics

Electronics are painful because students need laptops, monitors, calculators, routers, headphones, and phones.

But electronics also bring higher risk:

- Scams
- Broken devices
- Stolen goods
- Warranty disputes
- Higher-value transactions
- More support work

Competitors such as Blocket, Tradera, Refurbed, Swappie, and Inrego already cover parts of this market.

**Assessment:** good later category, not the safest first category.

**Reason:** demand is strong, but trust and fraud problems are harder for a solo founder.

## Best Category To Start With

Do **not** start with textbooks as the main wedge.

Textbooks are painful, but Sweden already has strong specialist competitors. It can still be included as a secondary category, but it should not be the whole positioning.

## Recommended First Wedge

Start with:

```text
Student dorm essentials marketplace:
small furniture, lamps, desks, chairs, kitchen items, routers, monitors, calculators, and basic electronics.
```

## Why This Is Better For A Solo Founder

- Easier to market around student move-in and move-out periods.
- More local and campus-specific than textbooks.
- Less dominated by specialist competitors.
- Easier to seed manually through student groups.
- Can begin with pickup-only transactions.
- Lets you build trust around verified students and campus proximity.
- Gives you a wider product range without becoming a general marketplace immediately.

## Positioning

Use this positioning:

```text
A trusted student marketplace for buying and selling dorm essentials near your campus.
```

Alternative:

```text
Buy and sell student essentials safely with students near you.
```

## MVP Category Scope

### Start With

- Small furniture
- Lamps
- Desks and chairs
- Kitchen items
- Storage boxes
- Routers
- Monitors
- Calculators
- Basic electronics
- Textbooks as a secondary category

### Avoid At First

- Large sofas
- Beds and mattresses
- High-value laptops and phones
- Clothing/fashion
- Delivery logistics
- Full payment escrow
- Multi-city launch

## Validation Plan

Before building too much, validate demand with students directly.

### Questions To Ask Students

1. What did you need to buy when you moved into student housing?
2. Where did you look first?
3. What was annoying, risky, or expensive?
4. Have you bought from Blocket, Facebook Marketplace, or student groups?
5. What would make you trust a student marketplace more?
6. What items would you sell before moving out?
7. Would campus verification make you more likely to buy?
8. Would you use a marketplace only for students near your campus?

### Signals To Look For

- Students mention moving costs repeatedly.
- Students complain about scams or unreliable sellers.
- Students already use Facebook groups or Blocket but dislike the experience.
- Students have unused items they want to sell.
- Students say campus/student verification would increase trust.
- Students can name specific items they need or recently bought.

## Solo-Founder Recommendation

The MVP should not compete head-on with every Swedish marketplace.

It should compete on a narrow promise:

```text
Trusted student-to-student trading for campus essentials.
```

Your first product should prove:

- Students will list items.
- Students will contact sellers.
- Students will complete trades.
- Students trust verified student profiles.
- Students return during move-in and move-out periods.
